Should You Get Another Pet After Loss?

6 min read · August 2026

It's one of the most tender questions in grief, and often one of the most guilt-laden: is it okay to love another pet? Maybe people are already asking when you'll get "a new one," and it makes you flinch. Or maybe part of you longs for that companionship again — and another part feels like wanting it is a betrayal. Both feelings are normal. Let's walk through it gently.

There's no "right" timeline

Some people bring home a new pet within weeks and find it healing. Others need months, or years, or never feel ready again — and that's okay too. The right time isn't a date on the calendar. It's a feeling, and only you can recognize it. Don't let anyone rush you, and don't let anyone shame you for being ready sooner than they expected.

A new pet is not a replacement

This is the fear that stops many people: that adopting again means forgetting, or replacing, the one they lost. It doesn't. Love isn't a fixed amount that gets used up. Opening your heart to a new companion doesn't close the door on the old one — it simply proves that the love your first pet taught you was real enough to give again.

"A new pet doesn't take the place of the one you lost. They inherit the love that one taught you how to give."

Signs you might be ready

You may be nearing readiness if:

— You think of your lost pet with more warmth than raw pain
— You find yourself missing the daily rhythm of caring for an animal
— You can imagine a new pet as their own individual, not a stand-in
— The excitement, when it comes, feels bigger than the guilt

Signs it might be worth waiting

It may help to give yourself more time if:

— You're hoping a new pet will make the grief stop (it won't erase it)
— You're looking for an exact copy — same breed, same color, same name — to recreate what you lost
— You're doing it mainly to satisfy other people, or the children, before you're ready yourself
— The thought brings more anxiety than comfort

If you have other pets grieving too

Remember that surviving pets grieve as well, and sometimes a new companion helps the whole household heal — while other times it adds stress. Watch their cues, and give everyone time to adjust.

Honor the first, welcome the next

Many people find that keeping their first pet's memory alive actually makes room for a new one. When your lost companion has a settled place in your heart and your home — a photo, a keepsake, a story you still tell — welcoming a new pet feels less like replacing them and more like continuing what they began.
