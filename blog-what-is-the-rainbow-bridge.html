What Is the Rainbow Bridge?

5 min read · August 2026

If you've recently lost a pet, you've probably heard someone say they've "crossed the Rainbow Bridge." It's a phrase that shows up on sympathy cards, in support groups, and in the quiet things people say when they don't know how else to comfort you. But what does it actually mean — and why has it brought peace to so many grieving hearts?

The idea, simply put

The Rainbow Bridge is a gentle metaphor for the place pets are imagined to go after they die. In the story, just this side of heaven lies a beautiful meadow where pets who were loved run and play, healthy and young again, free of pain. They're happy — except for one small thing: they miss the person they left behind.

And so they wait. Until, one day, their person comes. In that reunion, pet and human cross the bridge together — and are never parted again.

"They are running and playing together, but the day comes when one suddenly stops and looks into the distance. It is you they have been waiting for."

Where the story comes from

The Rainbow Bridge comes from a prose poem written around the 1980s. Its exact authorship has long been debated, and for years it was shared anonymously — passed hand to hand among grieving pet owners, photocopied in vet offices, read aloud at goodbyes. That quiet, word-of-mouth spread is part of its power: it belongs to everyone who has ever needed it.

The imagery draws on the old symbolism of the rainbow as a bridge between earth and sky, between here and beyond — a thread found in many cultures long before the poem existed.

Why it brings comfort

You don't have to take the Rainbow Bridge literally for it to help. What it really offers is a way to hold two unbearable truths at once: that your pet is gone, and that the love between you didn't end. It gives grief an image to rest on — your companion whole and happy again, and the promise that goodbye isn't forever.

For many people, that image is exactly what they need on the hardest nights: not a theology, just a small, kind story that says the bond mattered, and it endures.

Ways people use it

The Rainbow Bridge has become part of how we memorialize pets. People read the poem at their pet's goodbye, include it in memorial cards, mark an "adoption day" or passing anniversary with it, or simply whisper "cross gently" when it's time. However you use it, it can be a small ritual that gives your grief somewhere to go.

Keeping them close, here

Whatever you believe waits beyond, there's also a bridge you can build right here: the memories you keep. The photos, the funny habits, the story of how you found each other — these are how a pet stays present in your daily life, long after they've crossed.
